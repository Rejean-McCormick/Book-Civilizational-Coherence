# Corpus Autonomy

## Question

What must remain possible if the founder is unavailable, disagrees with later maintainers, changes position, or becomes an unreliable authority?

## Independence condition

A mature corpus should remain:

- understandable;
- inspectable;
- executable where software exists;
- teachable;
- contestable;
- forkable where rights permit;
- maintainable without unique founder knowledge;
- capable of preserving provenance while diverging from later founder interpretation.

\[
CorpusContinuity \neq FounderContinuity
\]

## Forking the founder conceptually

A community may preserve an earlier principle or implementation while rejecting a later statement by the founder.

This is not paradoxical. It is a normal consequence of treating claims, versions and implementations as independently inspectable artifacts.

\[
FounderAtT_1 \neq BindingAuthorityAtT_2
\]

The useful question is not "What does the founder currently want?" but:

> What do the documented principles, evidence, rights, interfaces and governance procedures support?

## Leaving the throne

Founder decentering can include deliberate reduction of the founder's interpretive centrality once enough documentation, implementation and community capacity exist.

The objective is not disappearance for its own sake.

It is to create space for the corpus to be evaluated, criticized, adapted and transmitted without every question collapsing back into founder biography or intention.

## Success condition

A strong test is whether a competent outsider who has never spoken with the founder can:

1. locate authoritative documentation;
2. distinguish theory from implementation and narrative;
3. reproduce or operate relevant components;
4. identify unresolved claims;
5. disagree without losing access;
6. create a traceable fork;
7. teach the system to another person.
