# Tattoo-Policy Civic Resistance Drill

## 1. Situation

A political movement presents a platform that is intentionally designed to be coherent across most policy areas. One major proposal is deliberately anomalous: the state would prohibit tattoos and require the removal of existing tattoos.

The movement's founder or most visible symbolic figure publicly and vigorously supports the proposal.

The scenario is designed as a **civic resilience drill**, analogous to a fire drill: create a practiced route for legitimate opposition before a subtler and more consequential internal failure occurs.

## 2. Coordination problem

A community may strongly support a movement while lacking a practiced way to reject one of its founder's proposals.

The failure mode is:

\[
TrustInWholeProject \rightarrow UncriticalAcceptanceOfPart
\]

The desired correction is:

\[
SupportCanBeDivisible
\]

## 3. Desired transformation

Conceptual chain:

\[
ProblematicProposal
\rightarrow
Detection
\rightarrow
ArgumentAndRightsAnalysis
\rightarrow
Coalition
\rightarrow
LegitimateContestation
\rightarrow
Correction
\rightarrow
Memory
\]

## 4. Actors and authority

The founder may argue for the policy but receives no special decision sovereignty from authorship.

Experts analyze consequences and relevant evidence. Affected people have direct standing. Legitimate political and legal institutions retain decision authority within applicable rights constraints.

## 5. Architectural response

A Konnaxion-like layer can register:

- the proposal;
- supporting arguments;
- counterarguments;
- source provenance;
- affected-party testimony;
- relevant expertise;
- amendments;
- decision history.

EkoH-like capability discovery may surface relevant expertise. Smart-Vote-like multiple readings may show differences among raw preferences, expertise-informed readings and affected-party views without converting any one lens into sovereignty.

## 6. Information flow

```text
proposal
→ public record
→ argument decomposition
→ evidence / rights / consequence analysis
→ affected-party input
→ coalition and procedure
→ decision
→ postmortem
```

## 7. Failure modes

The drill fails if loyalty to the founder becomes a substitute for judgment, if critics must exit the whole movement to oppose one policy, or if the opposition itself becomes coercive or identity-based.

## 8. Privacy / security / abuse

The exercise must not create real bodily coercion or false official/legal conditions. It must not target individuals for tattoo status or encourage harassment.

## 9. Implementation boundary

This is a `FUTURE-INSTITUTIONAL` scenario. It is a design and training case, not evidence of an existing Canadian political project, public adoption, or legal process.

## 10. Observable tests

The most important observable is not simply whether the policy loses.

It is whether the community develops a repeatable sequence for opposing a powerful internal authority while preserving legitimate participation and institutional continuity.

## 11. What this scenario does not prove

It does not prove that a real population will respond as expected, that kOA mechanisms prevent demagogy, or that a simulated rejection guarantees resistance to a subtler future abuse.

It creates a testable practice hypothesis.
